*Work in progress.*

Burmese Days depicts Burma, now Myanmar, at the height of the British Raj. Orwell lays bare the full extent of the Raj’s infamous racism: inequality runs so deep that some Burmese come to believe themselves genuinely “inferior” to the British. He perfectly captures the sweeping, humid vistas of upper Burma and the wealth of wildlife that the greed of colonial trade has not yet stripped away. John Flory, our protagonist, is a British national whose long years in the country have left him entranced by its people, culture and landscape -- a love deepened by his loneliness and longing for company. But the racial hierarchy that governs colonial life leaves him no one to share it with, until the arrival of Elizabeth. Meanwhile, a sinister scheme is unfolding. U Po Kyin, a corrupt Burmese magistrate, is plotting to discredit Flory’s friend Dr Veraswami and manoeuvre his own way into the all-white European Club.
