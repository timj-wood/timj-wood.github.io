*Work in progress.*

Burmese days depicts Burma, now Myanmar, at the peak of the British raj's rule. The full extent of the raj's infamous racism is illustrated; racial inequality rages throughout the country to the point where some Burmese locals believe themselves to be "inferior" to the British. Orwell perfectly captures the sweeping, humid vistas which lie deep in the lands of Asia, and the vast arrays of wildlife present before the capitalist greed of trade fully engulfed the territories. John Flory, our protagonist and a British-national living in Burma, becomes fully entranced in the people, culture, and lands due to his long tenancy in the country - exacerbated by his loneliness and longing for company. The racist social normalities deeply rooted in the countries culture, led by the British, prevented this: until the arrival of Elizabeth. Mean whilst, a sinister plot develops to overthrow the local town government, led by U Po Kynin, a Burmese local weaved into the webs of Western greed.
