title: Introgression and selection in Scottish wildcats

This study explores how natural selection and introgression affect genomic variation in the European wildcat (Felis silvestris), a species threatened by hybridisation with domestic cats. Genome-wide SNP data from 108 cats was analysed using the pcadapt package to identify genes disproportionately influencing population structures, giving 1,275 candidate intervals for functional annotation. Eleven were functionally annotated, with seven of them (TRPC4, SLC6A6, SLC6A11, SLC6A1, PDE4B, CACNA1H, and CACNA1E) having strong outlier peaks, allowing further interpretation. Gene ontology (GO) molecular functions were GABA transport, taurine transport, and calcium ion signalling, suggesting that behavioural and metabolic pathways might be under selection. Principal component analyses (PCAs) showed patterns of introgression, local adaptation, and regional divergence. Population history and linkage disequilibrium were limiting factors, but the genes showed plausible genomic regions for changing wildcat ecology. These results may highlight potential markers and offer useful insights for conservation efforts, though further testing would be needed.
